TITLE:
How Big Is the Universe, Really?

DESCRIPTION:
The universe is 13.8 billion years old, yet the edge of what we can see is about 46 billion light-years away in every direction. How is that possible? We climb a ladder of light, from the Moon (1.3 light-seconds) to Voyager 1 (1 light-day), the Milky Way, Andromeda and the Laniakea supercluster, all the way to the edge of the observable universe. Then we find out why space stretches under light, why most of what we see we can never reach, how many galaxies there really are, and whether the universe has an end at all.

Sources
- NASA Science, Beyond Our Solar System: the Milky Way, about 100,000 light-years across, at least 100 billion stars
- NASA Science, Voyager 1: What Is a Light-Day (one light-day on Nov 18, 2026)
- Tully et al. (2014), Nature: The Laniakea supercluster of galaxies (~520 million light-years, ~100,000 galaxies)
- JWST "Mirage or Miracle" survey: MoM-z14 confirmed ~280 million years after the Big Bang (2025)
- Planck Collaboration (2018): cosmological parameters (age 13.8 billion years, flat space, H0 ≈ 67)
- Riess et al., SH0ES team: local Hubble constant ≈ 73 km/s/Mpc (Hubble and Webb data)
- NASA / Johns Hopkins APL / STScI (2021): New Horizons measures the cosmic optical background, hundreds of billions of galaxies rather than 2 trillion
- Krauss & Scherrer (2007), The Return of a Static Universe and the End of Cosmology
- NRAO, Ask an Astronomer: age vs diameter of the universe (46.5 billion light-year radius)
- Own calculations with the standard ΛCDM model (Planck 2018 parameters): particle horizon ~46 billion ly, Hubble radius ~14.5 billion ly, event horizon ~16.6 billion ly, MoM-z14 then ~2.2 / now ~34 billion ly, CMB gas then ~41 million / now ~45 billion ly, and the "universe shrunk to Earth" scale

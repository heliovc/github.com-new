TÍTULO: What Happens If You Fall Into a Black Hole?

DESCRIÇÃO:
Fall into a small black hole and you're torn apart before you even reach it. Fall into a giant one and you cross the point of no return without feeling a thing. We dive into both, side by side: a 10-Sun black hole and Sagittarius A*, the monster at the center of our galaxy. How strong the stretch gets, what your friends would see from far away, and exactly how much time you'd have left inside each one.

Sources
- NASA Science, 10 Questions You Might Have About Black Holes (spaghettification; a same-mass black hole would not change planetary orbits; Sgr A* about 4 million Suns, 26,000 light-years; M87 black hole 6.5 billion Suns)
- NASA Goddard Space Flight Center (May 2024), black hole plunge visualization by Jeremy Schnittman: 4.3 million solar masses, horizon about 25 million km, ~3-hour fall, camera torn apart 12.8 s after crossing, a 6-hour trip near the horizon leaves the astronaut 36 minutes younger
- Event Horizon Telescope, April 10, 2019 (M87*: 55 million light-years, 6.5 billion solar masses) and May 12, 2022 (Sagittarius A*)
- LIGO Caltech, GW150914 (2015): black holes of about 36 and 29 solar masses merging 1.3 billion light-years away
- ESA Gaia (April 2024): Gaia BH3, 33 solar masses, about 1,926 light-years
- El-Badry et al. (2023), MNRAS: Gaia BH1, about 1,560 light-years
- Hawking (1974), Nature: "Black hole explosions?"
- Own calculations (Schwarzschild metric): horizon sizes (59 km, 25.4 million km, 38.4 billion km), tidal stretch 2GML/r³ on a 2 m body (1 g at ~8,150 km and 100 g at ~1,760 km from a 10-Sun hole; ~21 million g at its horizon; ~1/9,000 g at the Sgr A* horizon), maximum time from horizon to singularity πGM/c³ (0.15 ms, 66 s, 27.9 h), evaporation time ≈ 2×10^67 years for one solar mass
